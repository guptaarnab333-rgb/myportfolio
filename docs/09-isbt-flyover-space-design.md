## Site

**Dehradun's ISBT Flyover** — connects Transport Nagar to the Shimla bypass.

| Milestone | Detail |
|---|---|
| Foundation | March 2013 |
| Inauguration | December 11, 2016 |
| Cost | ₹50.39 Crore |
| Nearby localities | Turner Road, Majra (near Haridwar Bypass), Clement Town (with Tibetan Mindrolling Monastery) |

History: complex and delayed construction — pushed back significantly from the original March 2015 opening date due to political issues (President's rule in the state) and construction challenges. Even after the main flyover opened, service-road work continued — disrupting local residents.

## Scope of Design Work

| Included | Excluded (Design Boundaries / Limitations) |

## Research Process

**Goal:** Understand pain points, emotional needs, and aspirational needs of users before jumping into design.

- **On-site observation** — user behaviour, traffic flow, problem spotting
- **Stakeholder interviews** — locals, students, shopkeepers, drivers, travellers
- **Documentation & clustering** — extracting patterns across responses

### Project Start — Initial Brief
> *"To redesign the under-flyover space in ISBT, Dehradun into a sustainable and inclusive public space that responds to the needs of the client and the local stakeholders."*

### Project Plan (6 steps)
1. Site study & user interviews
2. Research synthesis & zoning concepts
3. Concept sketches & form development
4. Final layout
5. 3D modelling & prototyping
6. Model building + material testing

## Stakeholders Identified

- Local business owners
- Local residents
- Auto / Taxi / Delivery drivers
- Working professionals
- Daily commuters

### Interview Questionnaire (sample categories)

**General questions:** Frequency of passing through, informal use of the space, main concerns, most useful function if redesigned, absolute deal-breakers, desired facilities.

**Locals:** Seating, exercise spaces, shaded socializing, impact on daily life, neighbourhood quality, problems faced (noise, pollution, safety, garbage).

**Taxi / Auto Drivers:** Need for washrooms, drinking water, charging points; parking use; how flyover affects business.

**Kids:** Fun activities under the flyover.

**Passersby:** Safety perception, commute patterns.

**Youth:** Hangout spot potential, sports activities.

## User Personas (6)

### Priya Mehta — 22, Student
**Quote:** *"Uses the underpass occasionally while coming to or from ISBT."*
- **Goals:** Wants the underpass to function as a welcoming transit hub — organized, comfortable, safe. Hopes for shaded seating, clean surroundings, visible signage, basic amenities.
- **Frustrations:** No shaded, comfortable waiting space · Lack of information or signage · Poor lighting and unclear circulation · Feels unsafe, especially at night or when alone.
- **Behaviors:** Waits under the flyover while arranging transport or meeting friends. Often seeks shelter from sun/rain. Finds the area confusing, noisy, sometimes unsafe.

### Rakesh Singh — 34, Office Worker (Dehradun, Married)
**Quote:** *"Passes through the underpass daily while commuting or walking to work."*
- **Goals:** Wants the underpass to become a clean, well-lit, safe public route. Hopes for green buffers, shaded paths, seating to make it pleasant.
- **Frustrations:** Dark, unhygienic, unsafe environment · No defined pedestrian pathway · Unpleasant smell and cluttered parking.
- **Behaviors:** Uses the space mainly to cross the road or reach nearby shops. Avoids the area at night. Prefers quick, safe, shaded routes.

### Nicki Sahu — 24, Office Worker (Dehradun, Single)
**Quote:** *"Passes through frequently to go to office."*
- **Goals:** Dust-free, green and clean space · Easier access to public transport · Shelters for the homeless.
- **Frustrations:** Feels unsafe at night · Too much dust and pollution · Hard to wait for public transport due to traffic, lack of cleanliness and sitting area.
- **Behaviors:** Waits for public transport or other people under the flyover. Doesn't feel safe during the night. Often carries lots of stuff while standing.

### Sajid Ali — 31, Taxi Driver (Dehradun, Married)
**Quote:** *"Uses the underpass to park the vehicle and pick passengers."*
- **Goals:** Seating area for waiting passengers · Green and cleaner parking area.
- **Frustrations:** No public washrooms nearby · Abundance of dust, traffic, noise pollution.
- **Behaviors:** Parks under the flyover to look for vehicles/passengers. Has to travel far for washrooms. Often hard to enter the area due to traffic.

### Vishesh — 16, High School Student (Dehradun)
**Quote:** *"I want to have water dispensers under the void area."*
- **Goals:** Play with friends in green space · Water dispensers (esp. during summers) · Safe space to stand and wait for auto/bus.
- **Frustrations:** Unavailability of water · No designated standing area · Crowded space with no area to play.
- **Story:** Class 12 student, daily traveller by auto, active in sports, plays badminton daily, often waits with friends.

### Pramod Negi — 60, Shopkeeper (Pauri Garhwal)
**Quote:** *"I would love to have peaceful and clean environment outside my shop."*
- **Goals:** Peaceful road · Clean underpass with urinals · Less dust and noise.
- **Frustrations:** Traffic noise · Dust around his shop · Has to leave shop unattended due to no public urinals.
- **Story:** Old-age shopkeeper from humble background. Has been there for years, watched the flyover being built. Covered his shop with glass doors due to dust/noise. Simple, optimistic life. Has expectations from the coming generation.

## Affinity Mapping

Brainstormed ideas were sorted using the **affinity mapping method**, then clustered into priority themes:

- **Safety & Hygiene**
- **Charging / Waiting / Water / Toilets**
- **Shade & Climate Relief**
- **Comfort & Seating**
- **Sports / Recreation / Socializing**

> *These clusters guided the zoning of the space and helped prioritise features based on real demand.*

## Observed Problems

| Category | Issues |
|---|---|
| **Hygiene & Maintenance** | Space unhygienic & littered · Feels unsafe especially for women · Poor drainage, smell issues |
| **Lack of Amenities** | No public toilets or water · No seating or shaded rest · No charging or waiting areas |
| **Missing Greenery & Comfort** | Harsh microclimate, no trees · No softscape or visual relief · Unpleasant for daily users |

> *"Users perceive the under-flyover as neglected, unsafe, and uncomfortable, lacking identity and human scale."*

## ⭐ THE BIG PIVOT — Under → Over

> *"We shifted our project's focus from under the ISBT flyover to the unused space over the flyover. Our goal is to design this overhead area to meet stakeholder requirements."*

### Redefined Design Brief
> *To design a multi-level public space on & around the ISBT flyover that transforms urban space into an interactive public area and fosters social inclusivity and community engagement.*

## Material Selection

> **A lightweight carbon-fibre skeleton carrying a breathable, translucent skin — designed for sustainability, sensory comfort, and the future skyline of Dehradun.**

### Roof & Primary Skeleton
- ETFE membrane (translucent skin)
- Carbon-fibre composite (primary structure)
- Integrated flexible solar film

### Secondary Structure & Enclosure
- Recycled steel
- Bamboo composite
- Perforated metal shading
- Weathered bamboo shading screen

### Flooring & Interiors
- Reclaimed wood
- Bamboo plywood
- Earth tiles
- Rubber flooring (kids area)

### Anchor & Landscape
- Local sandstone
- Exposed concrete
- Permeable paving
- Native flora & green walls

### Material Application Reference (1–7 mapped on the physical model)

1. **Roof Skeleton** — Carbon Fibre
2. **Roof Skin** — ETFE
3. **Perforated Façade** — Metal/Bamboo
4. **Indoor Floors** — Wood, Earth Tiles
5. **Kids Area Flooring** — Recycled Rubber
6. **Green Zones** — Native Vegetation
7. **Glass Envelope** — Low-E Glass

### Material System

| Zone | Materials |
|---|---|
| **Primary Structure** | Carbon fibre frame · Recycled steel connectors · Minimal-footprint steel anchoring points |
| **Roof System** | ETFE membrane panels stretched over the carbon-fibre ribs · Perforated metal / bamboo shading |
| **Walls / Enclosure** | Low-E glass · Bamboo composite panels |
| **Floors / Interiors** | Bamboo ply · Compressed earth tiles · Recycled rubber · Locally sourced sandstone & exposed concrete finishes in common areas |
| **Landscape** | Native vegetation integrated into planters · All surface coatings use low-VOC paints |

### Sustainability Highlights
- Lightweight carbon-fibre reduces load on the existing flyover
- ETFE roof allows natural light + reduces heat + is fully recyclable
- Recyclable + low-impact materials throughout

Scaling the drawings and the prototype in a **1 meter : 2 centimeter** ratio. Team huddled around large drawing sheets, drafting curves and dimensions.

Testing the possible techniques and materials that could be used in making the model — **starching fabric technique** explored for the canopy skin.

### Assembling the Model
Team assembled the model with the laser-cut pieces, fitting everything together. Photos show collaborative model-building in studio.

## Unique Angle

Reimagines wasted urban infrastructure — **the surface above a flyover** — into a community asset. Lightweight, sustainable, solar-integrated form that combines amphitheatre, kids park, indoor games, and amenities atop a high-footfall transit zone.
