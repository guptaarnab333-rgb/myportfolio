## 1. The Ignored User — Water Purifier & Dispenser for India's Construction Workforce

**Discipline:** Product Design (Social / Inclusive Design)
**Format:** 65-slide presentation

### Context
Daily-wage construction workers (men and women, 20–55) on Indian urban/semi-urban sites spend 8–12 hour shifts in extreme heat. Water arrives in tanker trucks, gets poured into open shared drums — unfiltered, uncovered, contaminated by hand-cupping and shared ladles. No purpose-built water infrastructure exists for this user. Existing office/home dispensers fail within weeks under site conditions.

### Problem Statement
Design a durable, low-energy water purifier and dispenser that survives harsh site conditions, requires no hand contact to operate, and serves clean water to the most ignored user.

### User Research (5W Framework)
- **Who:** Workers (no power to demand better) + contractors (sole decision-makers)
- **Where:** Active construction sites — dusty, loud, moving
- **When:** Peak demand mid-morning and post-lunch in Indian summers
- **Why nobody fixed it:** Workers have no voice; contractors face no market pressure
- **Current coping:** Endurance, not coping — dehydration normalized, waterborne illness routine

### Pain Points Identified
- Contamination at source · No hands-free interaction · One height fits nobody · Direct mouth/hand contact · No maintenance ownership · Borrowed products that don't belong on site

### Solution
A faceted (octagonal) steel unit — solar-powered, four-stage filtered, foot-operated, and mobile.

### Key Specifications
- **Two spout heights:** 120cm and 152cm (serves shorter workers/women and average adults; both usable for direct drinking or bottle filling)
- **Storage tank:** 1.7ft × 1.7ft × 2.5ft — 150+ litres per fill (serves 30–50 workers per shift)
- **Solar panel:** 1.5ft diameter octagonal, removable, 5m wire (~30–40W peak)
- **Battery:** 12V, 7–12Ah
- **Generation vs consumption:** Generates ~5× what it consumes (150 Wh/day vs 22–30 Wh/day)

### Filtration (Four Stages, Zero Waste Water)
1. **Sediment filter** — dust, mud, particulate
2. **Activated carbon** — chlorine, organic compounds, odour
3. **Ceramic filter** — bacteria and biological contamination
4. **UV lamp** — final sterilization before storage
*All cartridges are modular and tool-free replaceable.*

### Mechanism
- **Two independent DC pumps** (one per spout — no cross-contamination)
- **Foot paddle** → electrical switch → pump activation → instant flow. Release stops flow. **Zero hand contact.**
- **Retractable caster wheels** — deploy for repositioning, retract for stability

### Materials
- **Outer body:** Galvanised steel with thermally reflective powder coat (deflects solar IR)
- **All water-contact surfaces:** Food-grade stainless steel — spouts, taps, tank lining
- Minimal seams, minimal dirt traps

### Design Thesis
*"This is not a water purifier. It is a statement, that the person doing the most physically demanding work deserves the same basic dignity as anyone else."*

### Portfolio Framing
Lead with the narrative hook ("The Ignored User"). Strong social-impact case study — emphasize the 5W research framework, hands-free dispensing, and the dignity thesis. Renders + filtration diagram + sketch progression.

## 2. From Clutter to Comfort — Desk Organizer & Laptop Stand

**Discipline:** Product Design
**Course mentor:** Mr. Utsav Baluni
**Duration:** 4 weeks, individual project

### Context
Arnab's own hostel desk became cluttered from studying, eating, sketching, and storage — making it hard to stay organized and productive. Self-driven brief expanding to hostel students at large.

### Problem Statement
Hostelers face workspace cleanliness and organization issues due to limited work and storage space being used for multiple tasks, resulting in decreased productivity and clutter.

### Methodology
**Double Diamond** (Discover → Define → Develop → Deliver). 5W method, user journey maps, mind maps, benchmarks.

### Research Insights (Survey + Interviews)
- **91%** — lack of storage impacts productivity
- **100%** — current workspace state affects focus and task completion
- **73%** — find workspace cluttered or disorganized
- **64%** — find it challenging to keep workspace clean and organized
- Multi-use behavior: eating, watching films, painting, sketching alongside studying — switching tasks is frustrating
- **5 Whys analysis** to root-cause clutter

### Design Brief
A simple product that allows efficient and secure freeing-up of space and organization, eliminating the need to constantly move things from the desk while switching between tasks.

### Develop / Final Concept
- No hinges, no screws — designed from a **single metal sheet, bent to form**, jointless
- Iterated through 3 low-fidelity prototypes → mid-fidelity → final

### Testing & Feedback
Real-world before/after testing. Feedback loop included:
- Reduce sharp corners and edges
- Use less material
- Add holes on middle portion and sides
- Use thinner sheet
- Improve form flow

### Takeaway
*"This project taught me the value of user-centered design — creating practical solutions that improve workspace organization and productivity for hostel students."*

### Portfolio Framing
Tight, scoped, well-structured Double Diamond case study. Good for showing methodology fluency early in the design timeline. Show the single-sheet-bent concept clearly — it's the most distinctive moment.

## 3. Redesigning Headway — Expanding Accessibility & Engagement

**Discipline:** UX/UI Redesign
**Platform:** Android | **Duration:** 2 weeks | **Scope:** 50+ screens
**Subject:** Headway (book summary app)

### Why Redesign
Outdated visual style, limited customization, English-only, weak audiobook UX, low engagement.

### Process
Discover → Define → Ideate → Design → Test

### Discover
- Competitor analysis: Blinkist, deepstash, Shortform, Headway, Google Play Books
- User survey: device habits, reading frequency, recommendation preferences, language needs, distractions
- **Key findings:** Most users want multi-language (especially Hindi), get distracted while reading, want personalized recommendations

### Define — Two Personas
- **Aanya** — confusing login (too many options, no hierarchy), overwhelming onboarding cards, vague feature explanations, lengthy interest-selection with unclear payoff
- **Rajesh** — poor reading experience (unclear summary length, weak readability, limited color modes, no playback-speed control); limited post-reading interaction (no highlights, notes, related content)

### Problem Statement
Headway's UI and navigation make information/features hard to find, reducing engagement, satisfaction, and retention.

### Five Core Challenges
Complicated navigation · Lengthy onboarding · Interruptive streaks · Restricted content discovery · Lack of customization

### Ideate — Old vs New Features
| Old | New |
|-----|-----|
| 1 font size only | 4 fonts |
| Light + Dark only | Light, Dark, Beige, Black |
| Audiobook (basic) | + White noise (rainfall, gentle waves) |
| No reviews | Ratings & Reviews |
| Library w/o customization | Custom reading lists |
| English only | Multiple languages |
| Translate prompt | Built-in dictionary |
| Streak prompts | Streak animations |
| — | Annotation sharing |

### Design
- **Typography:** Roboto (UI) · Cartier Book Std + Times New Roman + Open Sans (reading modes)
- **Tokens:** Spacing scale, stroke weights (1/1.5/2 px), corner radii (R1–R4)
- **Color palette:** Primary, decorative, background sets
- **50+ screens** delivered: dashboard, favorites, categories, book detail, reading view with theme/font controls, audio player, streak animations, translation overlay, custom reading list

### Portfolio Framing
Strong UX-heavy case study. Show research → personas → problem → ideation table → high-fidelity screens. Style-guide page is a great visual interlude.

## 4. Feminist Co-Design of Everyday Spaces with Women's SHGs

**Discipline:** Design Research / Field Study
**Course:** Field Study II | **Guide:** Dr. Dhriti Dhaundiyal | **School of Design, Doon University**
**Team:** Group 2 — Arnab, Srija, Kanika, Vidushi, Deepanshu, Palina
**Date:** September 2025
**Participants:** Women of SEVA BHARAT SHG, Dehradun (5–6 women)
**Arnab's roles:** Facilitation (with Srija) + Media (with Kanika, Deepanshu)

### Context
SHGs (Self-Help Groups) are strong community networks. The study explored how *feminist participatory co-design* can help women express their daily lives and aspirations through visual/spatial storytelling.

### Objectives
Enable dialogue · Understand lived experiences · Identify challenges & opportunities · Surface aspirations · Create visual narratives

### Research Design
- **Approach:** Feminist participatory design — equal voice, valuing lived experience, avoiding top-down framing
- **Method:** Visual participatory — collage-making workshop + informal storytelling + group dialogue
- **Setting:** Community space (meeting hall, courtyard, Anganwadi)
- **Facilitation:** Non-intrusive, women-led, in simple Hindi (jargon-free); participants free to skip/adapt
- **Data sources:** Collages (primary artifacts), participant narrations (audio/video with consent), facilitator field notes

### Workshop Flow
1. **Warm-up & comfort building** (10–15 min) — chai/chat, explain activity, no "right or wrong"
2. **Collage creation** (30–40 min) — theme: *Dincharya* (a day in their life as homemakers)
3. **Sharing & dialogue** (20–30 min) — each woman narrates her collage, group asks gentle follow-ups

### Materials
A3 sheets, magazines, newspapers, glue sticks, scissors, colored paper, sketch pens, bindis/stickers, nail polish & cosmetics, fabric scraps, post-its

### Findings & Insights
- **Shared companionship** — SHGs offer friendship, laughter, emotional support
- **Empowerment through Seva** — financial confidence, decision-making, bookkeeping, negotiation, group coordination
- **Everyday mobility** — group travel (markets, banks, workshops) builds courage and safety
- **Group dynamics & routine** — saving, lending, household discussion integrate with personal conversation; work and emotion coexist
- **Space & togetherness** — hall/courtyard as zones of freedom and equality (sitting on the floor together = equality)
- **Reflection & expression** — collage activity helped them realize how much they do in a day; visual storytelling unlocked emotional openness

### Final Outcomes
1. **Collage — "A Day in the Life"** (daily routine mapping by participants)
2. **Sakhiyan — Short Documentary** (8-frame storyboard): title → SHG head intro → group activity intro → individual member intros → personal interviews → collage activity → collaborative making → final collage + group photo

### Portfolio Framing
Showcases empathy, ethical research, and team collaboration. A great counterweight to studio-led design projects — proves Arnab can run real qualitative research with vulnerable communities. Highlight the documentary thumbnail and a few collage shots.

## 5. Uphaar — Sustainable Tea Packaging Redesign

**Discipline:** Packaging Design
**Year:** 2026
**Subject:** Uphaar (a 77-year Assam tea brand, Dugar Group) · SKU: Golden Tips, 250g single-origin Assam tea (₹720)

### Brand
*Uphaar* = "gift" in Assamese. Founded 1948 (Shree Hanuman Bhandar). GTAC entry 1970. Packaged tea launched 1988. DCPL formed 1993. Today: 30+ year legacy in premium single-origin tea.

### Problem
1. **Generic packaging** — standard metalized poly pouches indistinguishable from competitors
2. **Plastic dependency** — incompatible with eco-conscious consumer values
3. **No cultural story** — fails to communicate Assam's heritage and the gift emotion
4. **Weak premium signal** — at ₹720/250g, the package doesn't justify the price

### Research
- **Target consumers:** Urban gifters (25–50, festival/corporate giving) · Conscious buyers · Tea connoisseurs · Culture seekers
- **Market data:** 78% urban consumers prefer eco-friendly packaging (Nielsen 2023) · ₹14,500 Cr Indian packaged tea market (2024) · 12% premium segment growth · 3× margin in gifting vs CTC
- **Category benchmark:** Tata Tea Gold, Wagh Bakri, Vahdam, Teabox, Makaibari Estate

### Design Insight
*"Tea pickers in Assam carry bamboo baskets — tokris — to collect freshly plucked leaves. What if the packaging itself was inspired by that basket?"*
A bamboo basket is the most authentic vessel Assam tea could come in. Bonus: bamboo is rapidly renewable, biodegradable, and reusable.

### Four Design Pillars
1. **Sustainability** — 100% plastic replaced; bamboo matures in 3–5 yrs vs 50+ for hardwood
2. **Cultural Heritage** — Sustains Assam's bamboo craft tradition and artisans
3. **Reusability** — Bamboo canister becomes a storage vessel after use
4. **Premium Identity** — Hand-woven bamboo gives unmatched shelf presence

### Anatomy
1. Kraft paper lid (printed brand logo)
2. Tear-to-open kraft collar (Uphaar script + origin story)
3. Hand-woven bamboo body (tokri-inspired)
4. Botanical illustration visible through the weave
5. Food-safe inner liner
6. Reusable container with explicit reuse instructions

### Visual Language
- **Typography:** Existing red logo retained · Bold caps for product name · Italic serif for provenance subtitle
- **Palette:** `#8B7355` Natural Bamboo · `#C09A5B` Bamboo Gold · `#E5DBC5` Kraft Cream · `#E3272C` Uphaar Red · `#677149` Assam Green
- **Material logic:** Zero lamination, FSC-certified kraft, compostable inner pouch

### Impact
- **Environmental:** 35% higher CO₂ absorption than hardwood; eliminates ~18g plastic/unit; fully biodegradable
- **Cultural:** 1,000+ years of bamboo craft heritage; supports artisans; UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage
- **Commercial:** Higher AOV (giftable premium); reusability extends brand presence in homes; bamboo story = PR; aligns with India's ₹1 lakh cr bamboo economy push

### Portfolio Framing
Visually dense, photography-driven case study. Lead with the *before* (generic pouch) → insight → *after* (woven bamboo prototype). Show the anatomy diagram, palette swatches, and lifecycle (basket → table → reused storage).

## 6. Form Studies 1 — Form Derivation Documentation

**Discipline:** Foundation / Form Studies
**Institution:** School of Design, Doon University
**Mentor:** Utsav Baluni

### Theme
Form derivation influenced by **Sound, Emotions, Nature, and Morphing Forms.**

### Concept
Translating intangible stimuli (sound, emotion, nature) into dynamic physical forms through materials, textures, and morphing. Focus on *transformation between forms* rather than static objects.

### Process
1. **Sound Interpretation Activity** — listened to a sound track and translated it visually
2. **Group activity** — scribbling and colouring to discover candidate forms
3. **Major form** — selected from the sound-driven exploration
4. **Minor form** — selected from nature inspiration
5. **Morphing study** — both forms shown transforming on a single sheet

### Material Explorations
- **Sunboard build:** Morphing both forms in sunboard, treated with sunboard dust + colour for textural depth
- **Thermocol build:** Morphed forms in thermocol, treated with white cement for wave-like texture, blue accents to evoke water/sound waves

### Outcome
Two finished morphing sculptures displayed in a final exhibition.

### Takeaway (own words)
*"Form Studies teaches how to turn ideas into creative designs using different materials, textures, and forms."*

### Portfolio Framing
A foundation/exploration page or a short side-study. Photo-led. Pair with Form Studies 2.

## 9. Space Design Project — ISBT Flyover, Dehradun

**Discipline:** Spatial / Architectural Design
**Institution:** School of Design, Doon University
**Team:** Arnab Gupta, Deepanshu N. Goswami, Palina Chandel, Aditya Musan, Anjali Sharma, Yuvika Chauhan
**Course Facilitator:** Mr. Harshit Taneja
**Hypothetical Clients:** MDDA / PWD

### Site
Dehradun's ISBT Flyover (foundation March 2013, opened December 11, 2016, ₹50.39 Cr). Connects Transport Nagar to Shimla bypass. Nearby: Turner Road, Majra, Clement Town.

### Project Scope
Spatial planning · Structural coordination · Material and finish selection · Landscape and environmental elements · Furniture & fixture · Wayfinding & accessibility.
*Excluded:* real construction, structural calculations, costing, electrical/plumbing.

### Research Process
- On-site observation · stakeholder interviews (locals, students, shopkeepers, drivers, travellers, kids, parents, youth, bike riders) · documentation & clustering
- **Personas built:** Priya Mehta (22, student), Rakesh Singh (34, office worker), Nicki Sahu (24, office worker), Sajid Ali (31, taxi driver), Vishesh (16, student), Pramod Negi (60, shopkeeper)
- **Affinity mapping clusters:** Safety & Hygiene · Charging/Waiting/Water/Toilets · Shade & Climate · Comfort & Seating · Sports/Recreation/Socializing
- **Observed problems:** Hygiene/maintenance · lack of amenities · missing greenery and softscape · users perceive under-flyover as neglected, unsafe, identity-less

### Major Design Pivot
Project shifted from designing space **UNDER** the flyover to designing space **OVER** the flyover — a multi-level public space atop the existing structure.

### Redefined Brief
*"To design a multi-level public space on & around the ISBT flyover that transforms urban space into an interactive public area and fosters social inclusivity and community engagement."*

### Proposed Facilities
- **Important:** Sports facility · Open amphitheatre · Children's park · Amenities complex
- **Integrated:** Waiting area · Seating complex · Parking complex
- **Not required:** Co-working · Stand · Study complex · Open gym · Art gallery · Open stage · Auditorium · Salons · Yoga studio

### Dimensions
- Compartment: 83ft × 36ft
- Total structure: **55m × 32.5m × 40.5m** (scale 1:50)

### Building Layout
- **Top view:** 5m parking + footpath + road + 17.5m central platform with pillars
- **First floor:** Seating · waiting · phone booth · charging ports · washrooms · lockers · vending · water dispenser · kids playing area · open gym · ramp · transition area · lifts/stairs
- **Second floor:** Center-stage amphitheatre with stepped seating + indoor games (foosball, pool, chess, table tennis, playroom tables)
- **Zoning:** Indoor Games · Transition Area (central platform) · Amphitheatre · Park & Kids Play Area · Utilities

### 3D Form
Sculptural canopy: two curving solar-paneled wing-roofs meeting at a central peak. Modeled in **Autodesk Maya**.

### Sensory Design
Moodboards for Sound · Sight · Smell · Touch.

### Materials & Sustainability
- **Roof skeleton:** Carbon-fibre composite (lightweight; reduces load on existing flyover)
- **Roof skin:** ETFE membrane (translucent, recyclable, natural light)
- **Secondary structure:** Recycled steel · bamboo composite · perforated metal / weathered bamboo shading
- **Flooring:** Reclaimed wood · bamboo plywood · earth tiles · recycled rubber (kids area)
- **Walls/enclosure:** Low-E glass · bamboo composite panels
- **Landscape:** Local sandstone · exposed concrete · permeable paving · native flora & green walls
- **Coatings:** Low-VOC paints
- **Power:** Integrated flexible solar film in roof
- **Ventilation:** Cross-flow design through glass walls on both floors

### Prototyping
- Scaling at 1m : 2cm
- **Material testing** — starching fabric technique
- **SVG drawings for laser cutting** — elevator booth, flyover pillars, tunnel pieces, tunnel entry gate
- Physical scale model assembly (team build)

### Unique Angle
Reimagines wasted urban infrastructure — the surface *above* a flyover — into a community asset. Lightweight, sustainable, solar-integrated form combining amphitheatre, kids park, indoor games, and amenities atop a high-footfall transit zone.

### Portfolio Framing
The biggest, most ambitious case study. Lead with the pivot story (under → over). Show before-site photos, persona snapshots, the affinity map, the 3D model, floor plans, and the physical model build sequence. Group-credit the team clearly.

## 13. Gargi AI — UX/UI Design

**Discipline:** Mobile App UX + UI
**Subject:** AI mentor app for women's empowerment, grievance redressal, and emergency access in Uttarakhand

### Scope
- **UX:** Eliminate friction; reduce cognitive load to zero
- **UI:** Visually serene, logical, mobile-first

### Research
Google Forms survey, n=100 randomly sampled respondents.

### Key Insights
- **85%** prefer an AI mentor for sensitive career/personal questions (guaranteed anonymity)
- **90%** rated "immediate access to locally relevant emergency and government helplines" a top-3 necessity
- **Only 35%** could reliably recall more than 2 Uttarakhand-specific contacts
- **78%** felt formal complaints get ignored/lost on email or paper channels
- **90%** said real-time grievance status tracking is essential to institutional trust

### Sitemap
Open App → Login → **Chat with Gargi (AI)** · **Grievance Portal** (file + track) · **Helplines** · **SOS**

### Design System
Primary / Neutrals / Semantic palettes + grid system

### Prototyped Screens
- Seamless onboarding (Login ID or Facebook/Google)

## 14. Personal Logo — "Arnab Creates"

**Discipline:** Logo / Brand Identity (Self)

### Origin
Name *Arnav* (Sanskrit) = *Ocean*. Growing up in Assam — surrounded by rivers and waterways — that meaning was geography, not abstraction.

### The First Instinct (and Why It Failed)
Drew a wave. Then rejected it:
1. **Literal symbolism** — too obvious; required zero translation
2. **No design language** — sat in visual isolation; didn't suggest creativity, craft, or process
3. **Unscalable** — noise at small sizes; decorative at large sizes
4. **Wrong subject** — described who he was *named after*, not what he *does* or how he *thinks*

### The Shift
> *"The surface of the ocean is just water. Dive deeper, and you find systems, patterns, life."*

That's the same reason he designs. Not to decorate surfaces — but to find what lives underneath. Good design isn't visible. It's felt. The gap between what something looks like and why it works.

### The Concept
**See what others miss.**
The mark is an `a` (his initial) with an **eye embedded inside it.**
The eye isn't decoration — it's the thesis: a designer's most important tool is not software or process, it's the ability to *see*.

### The Process — Subtraction
Earlier iterations had coloured shapes (red, green, blue) orbiting the letterform to "signal creativity." Each revision removed something — colours, shapes, container border — until what remained was the only thing that mattered: the eye inside the letter.

### The Mark Reads Three Ways
- **`a`** — initial / name
- **Eye** — the designer's gaze
- **Ocean** — vast on the surface, rewarding to those who look closely

### Portfolio Framing
This belongs in the portfolio's **About / Identity** moment — footer or about page. It IS the portfolio's identity story.
