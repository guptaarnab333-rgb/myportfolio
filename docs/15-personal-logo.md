## 01 / Origin

> **My name means Ocean.**

In Sanskrit, *Arnav* (अर्णव) translates to the sea — vast, deep, and in constant motion. Growing up in Assam, surrounded by rivers and waterways, that meaning wasn't abstract. It was geography. It was daily life.

When it came time to design a personal identity, the ocean felt like an obvious starting point.

*Perhaps too obvious.*

---

## 02 / The First Instinct → The Problems

**Draw a wave. Everyone does.**

| # | Problem |
|---|---|
| **01** | **Literal symbolism** — a wave for a person named 'ocean' is the first thing anyone would draw. It required zero translation. |
| **02** | **No design language.** The wave sat in visual isolation — it didn't suggest creativity, craft, or process. |
| **03** | **Unscalable.** At small sizes the wave became noise. At large sizes it felt decorative, not purposeful. |
| **04** | **Wrong subject.** It described who I was *named after*, not what I do or how I think. |

---

## 03 / The Shift

> *"The surface of the ocean is just water.*
> *Dive deeper, and you find systems, patterns, life."*

That's the same reason I design. **Not to decorate surfaces — but to find what lives underneath.**

> Good design isn't visible. It's felt. It's the gap between what something looks like and why it works.

The wave was the surface. I needed to go deeper.

---

## 04 / The Concept

> **See what others miss.**

The mark is an **'a'** — my initial — with an **eye embedded inside it**.

The eye isn't decoration. It's the thesis:
> *A designer's most important tool is not the software, not the process — it's the ability to see. To notice the detail others scroll past. To find the pattern in the noise.*

The ocean is vast. But it rewards those who look closely.

---

## 05 / The Process — *Subtract Until Only Truth Remains*

The first iterations had **coloured shapes — red, green, blue — orbiting the letterform**. They were meant to signal creativity. Instead they created noise.

Each revision removed something:
- The colours
- The shapes
- The container border

…until what remained was the only thing that mattered: **the eye inside the letter.**

---

## 06 / The Mark — *Arnab Creates*

The final mark reads three ways simultaneously:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **'a'** | My initial. My name. |
| **EYE** | The designer's gaze. |
| **OCEAN** | Vast on the surface. Rewards looking closely. |

## Portfolio Framing

This logo IS the portfolio's identity. It should NOT be presented as a standalone "case study" buried in the work section — it belongs as a **first-person moment** somewhere prominent.

### Recommended Placements (pick one or combine):

**Option A — About Page Hero**
The logo + this six-step story becomes the centrepiece of the About page. Scroll-driven sections walk through Origin → First Instinct → The Shift → The Concept → The Process → The Mark.

**Option B — Footer or Brand Strip**
The minimal mark appears as the site's persistent brand element (top-left or footer), and a short "see how it was made" link takes visitors to the full story on a dedicated page.

**Option C — Process Aside on the About Page**
The mark appears in the header. A side panel or accordion expands to show the six-step process for those curious about the symbolism.

### Recommended: Option A
Lead the About page with this story. It tells viewers **how Arnab thinks** before showing them what he's made. The narrative arc (literal → too literal → subtract → concept) is the same arc visible across his other case studies — *The Ignored User* (subtract until only the user's dignity remains), *Biroti* (subtract until only the pause remains), *Uphaar* (subtract until only the bamboo remains). The logo story is the portfolio's thesis.
