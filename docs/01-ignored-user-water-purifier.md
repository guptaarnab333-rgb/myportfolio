## Where It All Begins — Context of Use

Daily-wage construction workers in urban and semi-urban India spend 8–12 hour shifts in extreme heat with no access to clean drinking water. Water arrives via tanker trucks and sits in open, unfiltered, shared containers — or they drink tap water, unhygienic and without basic consideration for the user.

Workers, both men and women, access water with dirty or wet hands between physically demanding tasks. No purpose-designed water infrastructure exists for this context. This project proposes a durable, low-energy water purifier and dispenser — designed for the harshest conditions, the most ignored users, and minimal infrastructure dependency.

## The 5W Framework

### WHO is actually thirsty?
Daily-wage construction workers — men and women, aged 20 to 55, spending 8 to 12 hours under the open sky, building cities. Their hands are permanently dirty, wet, or physically wrecked by the time they need water. They don't own the site. They don't own the water. They have no voice to demand better.

The **contractor** stands behind them — the sole decision-maker for worker welfare, the one who chooses what gets bought and what doesn't.

### WHAT is really happening?
Most of the time they drink tap water, or water arrives on site in a tanker truck. It gets poured into open drums and left there, unfiltered, uncovered, shared by dozens of workers throughout the day. No purification. No hygiene control. No individual dispensing. Everyone's hands go into the same water.

Workers without personal bottles drink directly — hand-cupping, mouth-to-tap, or tilting the drum. The water that builds India's infrastructure is the same water making its workforce sick.

### WHERE does the problem live?
Active construction sites — urban and semi-urban India. Dusty. Loud. Physically brutal. The environment is harsh, and the site itself moves when the project ends. No designated rest zones, no hydration points, no shade. The water drum sits wherever the tanker last parked, not where workers need it most. This is one of the hardest places on earth to design for. And nobody has tried.

### WHEN do they need it most?
Every hour. In short, desperate bursts between physically exhausting tasks. Peak demand hits mid-morning and post-lunch, when the heat is at its worst and the body is already running on empty. Workers cannot leave their stations freely. Water access is opportunistic, not scheduled.

In Indian summers, this is not inconvenience. This is a health crisis happening every single day, on every single site, in plain sight.

### WHY has nobody fixed this?
Because the user has no power. A daily-wage worker cannot demand better conditions without risking their livelihood. The contractor bears the cost of welfare provisions and faces no market pressure to improve them. Existing products designed for offices and homes fail immediately in this environment and are never replaced.

The problem persists not because the solution is impossible. It persists because the user was never considered worth designing for.

### HOW are they coping right now?
Those who can, carry a personal bottle from home. Those who can't, drink from whatever is available. Shared drums. Common ladles. Direct hand contact with open containers. Workers drink less than they should because access is inconvenient and the water is visibly unclean. Dehydration on site is normalised. Waterborne illness is accepted as routine.

This is not coping. This is endurance. And endurance is not a design solution.

## Problem Statement

Construction workers on Indian sites are forced to drink from open, unfiltered, shared containers — with no hygiene, no dignity, and no infrastructure designed for them.

> Design a durable, low-energy water purifier and dispenser that survives harsh site conditions, requires no hand contact to operate, and serves clean water to the most ignored user.

## User Pain Points

| Pain Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Contamination at the source** | Open, unfiltered tanker water shared by everyone, protected by nothing. |
| **No hands-free interaction** | Taps, drums, levers — every interaction needs hands that are never clean. |
| **One height fits nobody** | Existing dispensers ignore ergonomic reality across worker heights. |
| **Direct drinking with no clean outlet** | Workers without bottles put hands/mouth directly on shared surfaces. |
| **Nobody owns the maintenance** | Filters clog. Tanks empty. No indicator, no assigned responsibility. |
| **The product doesn't belong here** | Solutions borrowed from another context break within weeks. |

## The Response

A water purifier and dispenser designed from the ground up for the construction site. Not adapted from somewhere else. Not borrowed from an office or kitchen. Built specifically for this environment, this user, and this problem.

**A self-sufficient, faceted steel unit — solar-powered, four-stage filtered, foot-operated, and mobile.** Clean water on demand. No hands. No expertise. No excuses.

---

## Components (top to bottom)

1. Solar panel
2. Battery
3. Four-stage filter stack
4. Two DC pumps
5. Storage tank
6. Foot paddle and wheel mechanism at the base

*Every component has one job. Nothing is redundant.*

## Materials

| Surface | Material | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer body** | Galvanised steel with thermally reflective powder coat | Single flat panels form each face of the octagonal body. Minimal seams. Minimal dirt traps. Reflective coating deflects solar IR — keeps water cooler during 8–12 hrs of direct sun. |
| **All water-contact surfaces** | Food-grade stainless steel | Spouts, tap bodies, internal tank lining. No compromise. |

*Industrial where it needs to be tough. Clinical where it needs to be clean.*

## Water Purification — Four Stages

Zero waste water produced at any stage. All cartridges modular and tool-free replaceable.

1. **Sediment filter** — strips suspended dust, mud, and construction particulate from incoming tanker water. First contact. Takes the most abuse.
2. **Activated carbon filter** — removes chlorine, organic compounds, and odour. Water becomes chemically clean.
3. **Ceramic filter** — microscopic physical barrier. Removes bacteria and biological contamination without power or chemicals.
4. **UV lamp** — final stage. Sterilises any surviving microbial content before water enters storage. Nothing reaches the tank unclean.

---

## Additional Technology

### Two DC Pumps (one per spout)
Each pump sits independently in the system, dedicated to its own spout. No shared flow path, no cross-contamination risk between dispensing points.

### Foot Paddle — Electrical Switch
A flat paddle at the base activates an electrical switch on press. The switch signals the corresponding pump to activate — water flows instantly from the spout above. Release → pump stops → flow stops. No handles. No hand contact. No contamination at the point of use.

### Retractable Caster Wheels (four)
Lever-operated at the base. Wheels deploy for site repositioning, retract for stable ground contact when stationary. Integrated into the octagonal platform.

---

## Water Storage

- **Tank:** 1.7ft × 1.7ft × 2.5ft — 150+ litres per fill
- **Filling:** Fed directly from the site tanker through a large threaded inlet port. Tanker's onboard pump pushes water in — no additional pumping required.
- **Storage condition:** Tank is sealed and enclosed within the body. No open surfaces. No contamination risk during storage.

---

## Water Flow

```
Tanker pump → top inlet → gravity pulls water downward
   ↓
Sediment filter → Carbon filter → Ceramic filter → UV lamp
   ↓
Clean water stored in sealed base tank

Foot paddle pressed → electrical switch → DC pump activates → spout → instant flow
Foot paddle released → pump stops → flow stops
```

Gravity handles filtration. The pump handles dispensing. The paddle controls the pump. The solar panel powers the battery. The battery powers everything electrical. **Each element does exactly one job.**

## Power System

| Component | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Solar panel** | 1.5ft diameter, removable. ~30–40W peak. 5m wire. Charges internal 12V battery continuously through the day. |
| **Battery** | 12V, 7–12Ah. Stores surplus charge for overcast periods. Powers two DC pumps and foot paddle switches. |
| **Consumption** | Two pumps running intermittently — estimated 22–30 Wh daily total. |
| **Generation** | Solar panel ~150 Wh daily. |
| **Ratio** | **System generates nearly 5× what it consumes.** |

*All figures are estimates — to be confirmed against supplier specifications.*

## The Final Statement

Every city in India is being built by people who cannot access clean water while building it.

> This is not a water purifier. It is a statement, that the person doing the most physically demanding work deserves the same basic dignity as anyone else.

The technology exists. The need is documented. The user has been waiting.

**This is what design is for.**
