## Overview

The School of Design at Doon University celebrated **International Design Day** with a two-part programme themed around the River Ganga — bringing together creativity, culture, and community in a meaningful dialogue about the role of design in preserving natural and cultural heritage.

The day began with a traditional **lamp-lighting ceremony** in the presence of Chief Guest Dr. Lokesh Ohri, Registrar Dr. Durgesh Dimri, and Dean Prof. R.P. Mamgain.

---

## Part 1 — *Adbhut Ganga* Photo Contest Exhibition

> **A Celebration of Creative Expression Dedicated to the Ganga**

### Time
Inaugurated 11:00 a.m.

### Purpose
A unique effort to bring to students' attention the **diversity, cultural significance, and human emotions** associated with the River Ganga.

### Participation
- Overwhelming response across age groups and educational backgrounds
- Participants captured the life-giving nature of the Ganga, religious rituals, serene currents, and everyday activities through their cameras
- Entries reflected the Ganga's changing moods — devotion, beauty, hustle and bustle, serenity

### Jury & Selection
A distinguished jury selected **8 outstanding photographs** from the entries received.

### Winner
**Shubhangi Walia** — Dev Bhoomi University — specially honoured for the winning photograph.

### Impact
This initiative provided a platform for students to showcase their creativity, and served as an effective medium to bring a new interpretation of the **conservation, importance, and beauty of the River Ganga** to the public.

---

## Part 2 — *River as Culture, River as Life* Session

### Opening
A traditional lamp-lighting ceremony.

### Keynote
**Prof. Dhriti Dhaundiyal**, Head of the School of Design, delivered a keynote on:
> *The power of optimism in design — and how nurturing respect for our heritage (both cultural and ecological) can instill a sense of pride and responsibility among young learners.*

### Featured Talk — *Ganga Katha*
**Dr. Lokesh Ohri** (cultural historian and environmentalist) delivered a featured talk on the **deep cultural and environmental significance of India's most sacred river.**

### Documentary Screening — *Aakash Se Sansar*
Following Dr. Ohri's talk, his short film **"Aakash Se Sansar"** was screened — the **first in a four-part documentary series** created by Dr. Ohri for the Namami Gange programme.

The film wove together folklore, mythology, and lived experiences of communities along the river, emphasizing the Ganga's role in the human journey from birth to death.

### Discussion
The session concluded with a lively discussion moderated by filmmaker **Vineeth Horo**, during which Dr. Ohri encouraged students to become **active stewards of river conservation**.

### Student Films
Later in the day, **third-year students of the School of Design** presented their own short films based on **ethnographic research in Rishikesh**. The films captured the stories of the diverse communities that contribute to the cultural tapestry of the holy city.

Their work — part of their coursework on ethnographic interviews — was well received and elicited thoughtful feedback from the panel of experts present.

### Closing Ceremony
Presided over by **Prof. Surekha Dangwal**, Vice Chancellor of Doon University, who:
- Commended the School of Design for its commitment to socially and environmentally responsible education
- Reiterated the relevance of the Namami Gange mission in shaping the values of the next generation
