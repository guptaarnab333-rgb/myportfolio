## What Are SHGs?

Women's Self-Help Groups (SHGs) are strong community networks. This study explored how **feminist co-design** can help women express their daily lives and aspirations creatively through spatial understanding.

---

## Objectives

- Enable dialogue
- Understand lived experiences
- Identify challenges & opportunities
- Surface aspirations
- Create visual narratives

---

## Research Design

| Dimension | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Design Approach** | Feminist participatory design — giving equal voice, valuing lived experience, avoiding top-down framing |
| **Method** | Visual participatory — collage-making workshop supported by informal storytelling and group dialogue |
| **Setting** | Community space where SHG women usually gather (meeting hall, courtyard, Anganwadi, etc.) |
| **Facilitation Style** | Non-intrusive, encouraging, women-led where possible. Participants free to skip, alter, or adapt activities |
| **Language & Communication** | Entirely in simple Hindi, avoiding jargon. Facilitators adapt prompts to participants' comfort level |

### Data Sources

- **Collages created by participants** (primary artifacts)
- **Participant narrations** of their collages (audio/video, recorded with consent)
- **Field notes** from facilitators

## Participants

- **Primary participants:** Women members of local SHGs (5–6 participants for manageability)
- **Specifically:** Women from SEVA BHARAT SHG in Dehradun

## Workshop Flow

### 1. Warm-up & Comfort Building (10–15 min)
- Informal chat, chai/snack if possible
- Explain activity in simple terms
- Assure them there is no "right or wrong" collage

### 2. Collage Creation (30–40 min)
- Participants make a collage together showing **"Dincharya"** (a day in their life as homemakers)
- Encourage mixing cut-outs with drawings and words

### 3. Sharing & Dialogue (20–30 min)
- Each woman explains her routine briefly in her own words
- They share their experience with the SHG
- Group can ask small follow-up questions (handled gently by facilitator)

## Insights & Learnings

### Shared Companionship
- SHGs offer friendship, laughter, and emotional support
- Meetings strengthen bonds and belonging

### Empowerment Through Seva
- The Seva has increased financial confidence and decision-making among women
- They've learned skills like bookkeeping, negotiation, and group coordination

### Everyday Mobility
- The women travel together for markets, workshops, and bank visits
- Group travel builds courage and safety

### Group Dynamics & Routine
- Tasks like saving, lending, or discussing household matters integrate with personal conversations
- Work and emotion coexist in these meetings

### Space & Togetherness
- The hall or courtyard is a space of freedom and comfort
- Sitting together on the floor builds equality

### Reflection & Expression
- The collage activity made them realize how much they do in a day
- Visual storytelling helped them open up emotionally and connect through shared struggles and joys
